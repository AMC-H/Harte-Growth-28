Local SEO for service businesses: where we start

Starting local SEO from scratch? This order gets you the first visible results within 6 to 12 weeks.

Local SEO comes down to three things Google needs to know: that you exist, where you work, and whether you're trustworthy enough to show to people searching nearby. Someone typing "plumber Utrecht" or "air conditioning Moraira" wants to call someone within minutes. If you're not on the map or near the top of the list at that moment, you don't exist for that customer.

Below are the five steps we go through with every new client. The order isn't random: each step builds on the one before. Start with step 5 and you're building on sand.

Step 1. Get your Google Business Profile right

This profile is usually the first thing a potential customer sees, even before your website. It shows up in Google Maps and in the map block at the top of the search results. For many service businesses it brings in more phone calls than the website itself.

What we check first:

  • Name, address and phone number exactly the same everywhere: on your profile, your website, Facebook, business directories and industry sites. Small differences, like a different phone number or "Street" versus "St.", make Google unsure who you are.
  • The right primary category. It carries the most weight. Pick the category that best describes what you sell, not the broadest one. Secondary categories fill in the rest.
  • A correct service area. Do you visit customers and not receive anyone at your address? Then hide your address and set the towns you work in.
  • Real photos of your work, your team and your van or workshop. No stock photos.
  • Up-to-date opening hours, including public holidays.
  • Services with their own description, in the words your customers use.

After that we keep the profile alive: new photos regularly and an update whenever something changes. A profile that hasn't moved in months looks abandoned.

Step 2. A separate page for each area you serve

Your Google profile sits in one place, but your customers are spread across several towns. That's why every area you serve gets its own page on your website. Each page has:

  • "service in town" as the title, for example "Air conditioning installation in Jávea";
  • 200 to 400 words of unique text about that town: which customers you have there, what questions come up, how quickly you can get there;
  • examples of jobs you did there, with photos;
  • a map and a clear button to call, message or request a quote.

The key word is unique. Copying the same text and only swapping the town name doesn't work. Google recognises it and ignores those pages.

Step 3. Reviews, on purpose

Reviews are the strongest trust signal you have, for Google and for the customer choosing between you and a competitor. Aim for ten or more reviews with an average of 4.5 stars within three months.

How to get them:

  • Ask in person, right after a job went well, while the customer is still happy at the door. Not through one generic email weeks later that nobody opens.
  • Make it easy with a direct link to your review form, by WhatsApp or on a card with a QR code.
  • Reply to every review, including the critical ones. Short, friendly and specific. Future customers are reading along.
  • Don't buy reviews or offer discounts in exchange for one. It's against Google's rules and can cost you your profile.

Step 4. The technical foundation

Invisible to visitors, essential for how Google judges your page. These are the four things we always sort out:

  • LocalBusiness markup (structured data) with your name, phone number, service area and opening hours, so Google knows those details for certain.
  • An up-to-date sitemap, submitted in Google Search Console, so new pages get found quickly.
  • Speed on mobile. Most local searches happen on a phone. A page that stays blank for three seconds loses you the customer.
  • A valid HTTPS certificate, so browsers don't flag your site as unsafe.

Step 5. Visible in ChatGPT and Gemini too

More and more people ask an AI assistant for a recommendation: "Which installer in Dénia is good?" Those answers are based on what the internet says about you. Mentions on authoritative sites in your industry and region therefore help not only in Google, but also in the answers from ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.

This is called GEO, generative engine optimisation. It builds on exactly the same trust signals as classic local SEO: a complete business profile, consistent details, good reviews and clear pages per service and town. Get steps 1 to 4 right and you've also laid the groundwork for visibility in AI search engines.

Five mistakes we see a lot

  1. A half-finished business profile, set up once and then forgotten.
  2. Different phone numbers or addresses on different sites.
  3. One "Service area" page listing twenty town names, instead of a page for each town.
  4. Waiting for reviews instead of actively asking for them.
  5. Stopping after four weeks because nothing shows yet, just before the effect kicks in.

How do you know it's working?

Don't just look at your ranking for one keyword. It shifts by the day and by where someone is searching from. Look at these instead:

  • Impressions and clicks in Google Search Console, per page;
  • Calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Google Business Profile;
  • Enquiries through your form and WhatsApp. That's what it's really about.

Frequently asked questions

How long does local SEO take to show results?

You usually see the first movement within 6 to 12 weeks. After that the effect keeps growing as long as your pages stay online and new reviews and mentions come in.

Do I need a physical address for a Google Business Profile?

No. If you work at customers' locations, you can hide your address and set the towns you serve instead.

How many town pages should I create?

One for each town where you actually work and want customers. Five strong pages with unique content beat twenty copies with only a different town name.

Can I do local SEO myself?
